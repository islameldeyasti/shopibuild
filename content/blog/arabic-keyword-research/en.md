**Short answer:** Arabic keyword research starts from **your customers' real language**, not translation. Use **Google autocomplete**, the **"People also ask"** box and **your customers' WhatsApp questions**, then **Google Search Console** once you have traffic. Sort every keyword by **search intent** (buy, compare or learn), and give each main keyword **one page**.

## Why Arabic keywords are different

- **Multiple spellings:** "شوبيفاي", "شوبفاي" and "Shopify"; "عبايه" and "عباية" (abaya).
- **Dialects:** "أبي" vs "أريد" (I want), "وين" vs "أين" (where). Product names are often colloquial.
- **Mixed Arabic and English:** "متجر Shopify دبي" (Shopify store Dubai) is a very common way to search.
- **Paid keyword tools** usually have weaker Arabic data than English data.

## Step 1: start from your customers

Before any tool, write down:
- The questions you get on WhatsApp and Instagram.
- The words customers use to describe your products.
- The problems your product solves.

Example for a perfume store: "دهن عود أصلي" (genuine oud oil), "عطر يثبت طول اليوم" (long-lasting perfume), "عطور شتوية" (winter perfumes), "هدية عطر للرجال" (perfume gift for men).

## Step 2: Google autocomplete

Type the start of a phrase into Google (in a private window, with your location set to the UAE) and note the suggestions:

- "دهن عود …" (oud oil…) → genuine, Cambodian, prices, Dubai
- "عباية …" (abaya…) → black, linen, formal, embroidered

Add letters after the phrase ("دهن عود ا", "دهن عود ب") to surface more suggestions.

## Step 3: "People also ask"

The **"People also ask"** box in Google is a goldmine for FAQs and articles. Each question can become:
- A question in the FAQ block on a collection page.
- An H2 in an article.
- A full article, if the question is big enough.

## Step 4: analyse the competition

Search your main keywords and see who ranks:
- **Freelancers and service listings?** A strong opportunity for real content.
- **Big stores with collection pages?** You need a stronger collection page.
- **Old or translated articles?** Write something newer and more accurate.

## Step 5: classify by intent

| Intent | Example | Right page |
|---|---|---|
| Buy | "دهن عود كمبودي دبي" (Cambodian oud oil Dubai) | Collection or product page |
| Compare | "أفضل دهن عود" (best oud oil) | Collection page with comparison content, or an article |
| Learn | "كيف أعرف دهن العود الأصلي" (how to tell real oud oil) | Blog article |

A common mistake: writing an article for a buying keyword, or using a product page for a learning keyword.

## Step 6: one keyword per page

Give each main keyword **one page** on your store. Two pages targeting the same keyword compete with each other and weaken both. Keep a simple map:

| Keyword | Page | Type |
|---|---|---|
| دهن عود كمبودي | /collections/cambodian-oud | Collection |
| كيف أعرف دهن العود الأصلي | /blog/real-oud-oil | Article |

## Step 7: Google Search Console

Once your content is live, **Search Console** shows the queries you actually appear for, how often and at what position. Look for:
- Keywords where you rank 8–20: improve the page and push it onto page one.
- Unexpected keywords: write dedicated content for them.

## Useful tools

- **Google** itself: autocomplete and "People also ask".
- **Google Search Console**: your real site data, free.
- **Google Trends**: compare interest between terms, and seasons such as Ramadan.
- **Google Keyword Planner**: approximate search volumes (needs an ads account).
- Paid tools such as **Semrush** and **Ahrefs** for competition and volume, though less accurate in Arabic.

## FAQ

### Should I write "شوبيفاي" or "Shopify"?
Both. Use the more-searched form in the title and the other naturally in the text.

### Should I target Gulf dialect or Modern Standard Arabic?
Write in clear Arabic, and use customers' actual words in product names and titles, even when colloquial.

### How many keywords should a page target?
One main keyword, with its variants and close terms used naturally.
